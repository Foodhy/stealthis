No menu

We don't print a menu.
Because we don't know yet.

The cook writes the night's progression on Monday morning, after seeing what's ready in the field. You'll get six courses — sometimes seven — built around three or four heroes from that week. There is always something fermented, something raw, and something cooked over embers.

A recent night, late August:

  1. i. Tomato water · basil oil First-pick green zebras, salted three days.
  2. ii. Charred eggplant · whey · sumac From the ash of last night's hearth.
  3. iii. Beet leaves · brown butter · hazelnut The leaves you usually throw away.
  4. iv. Stone-fired flatbread · garden herbs Ten kinds of herb — pick them yourself at the table.
  5. v. Charred carrot · miso · sunflower Carrots harvested at 5:30 this morning.
  6. vi. Cucumber granita · honey From our hive at the field edge.

The garden

Four acres, fourteen metres.

The kitchen looks straight onto the field. Whatever's on your plate was, on average, growing fourteen metres from where you're sitting four hours ago.

We grow on no-till beds with a heavy cover-crop rotation. No petroleum-based inputs, no plastic mulch, no synthetic fertilizer. We compost everything — including the bones from the rare meat course — and the result goes back into the field within ninety days.

  • 92 varieties of vegetable, year-round
  • 14 orchard trees · apples, plums, quince
  • 3 beehives at the field edge
  • 1 wood-fired hearth, indoor & outdoor
