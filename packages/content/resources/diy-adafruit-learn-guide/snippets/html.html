Build a desk weather lamp

A palm-sized lamp that reads temperature, humidity and pressure, then glows a colour that tells you what the next few hours look like — blue for cooling, amber for warming, magenta when the pressure drops fast.

RM Rin MarloweHardware writer Intermediate ~2 h build Updated 2026-07-24 25 min read

What you will build

  • A three-sensor weather core. One I²C breakout reports temperature, humidity and barometric pressure once per second.
  • A colour forecast. Twelve addressable LEDs blend between blue, amber and magenta based on a 3-hour pressure trend.
  • A tap-to-read mode. Touch the metal ring and the lamp pulses out the current temperature in short flashes.
  • No soldering iron required for the base build — every connection uses the included spring terminals.
Before you start

Flash the board with the SOLDERBOX bootloader (v4.2 or newer). Older bootloaders do not expose the NeoPixel timing fix this sketch relies on.

Polarity check

Power the lamp only after all five wires are seated. A reversed VIN/GND pair on the LED disc will pop the first pixel instantly.

Assembly

Work on a non-conductive surface. Each step takes a few minutes; nothing here is glued until step 5.

  1. Seat the terminal wing

    Press the spring terminal wing onto the board's female headers until the plastic bottoms out. It only fits one way — the USB-C cutout faces the same edge as the board's port.

  2. Mount the sensor away from the LEDs

    Stand the TriSense breakout on the short standoffs at the rear of the base. Keeping 25 mm between the sensor and the LED disc stops the pixels' own heat from skewing the temperature reading by a degree or two.

  3. Land the five wires

    Strip 7 mm, press the orange lever, insert, release. Tug each wire gently — a good spring terminal will not let go.

  4. Drop in the LED disc

    The disc sits face-up in the base ring with the DIN arrow pointing at the front seam. Route its wire through the side channel so the dome can sit flat.

  5. Twist on the dome

    Align the two lugs, push down 2 mm and rotate clockwise until it clicks. Do not force it past the stop.

  6. First power-up

    Plug in USB-C. You should get one white flash, then a slow blue breathe while the sensor takes its first three readings.

Tip

If the dome rattles, add the thin foam ring from the kit bag between the base lip and the diffuser.

Code

Drop weatherlamp.py onto the board's USB drive. It runs on boot; no build step.

weatherlamp.py CircuitScript · 41 lines 
import time, board, trisense, glowring

SENSOR = trisense.THP(board.I2C(), address=0x76)
RING   = glowring.Disc(board.D6, 12, brightness=0.35)

# hue targets: cooling / steady / warming
COOL    = (43, 140, 255)
STEADY  = (184, 255, 61)
FALLING = (255, 61, 129)

history = []

def blend(a, b, t):
    return tuple(int(x + (y - x) * t) for x, y in zip(a, b))

def trend(samples):
    # hPa change across the last three hours
    if len(samples) < 2:
        return 0.0
    return samples[-1] - samples[0]

while True:
    temp = SENSOR.temperature
    hum  = SENSOR.humidity
    pres = SENSOR.pressure

    history.append(pres)
    if len(history) > 180:
        history.pop(0)

    delta = trend(history)
    if delta < -1.5:
        colour = FALLING
    elif delta > 1.5:
        colour = blend(STEADY, COOL, 0.4)
    else:
        colour = STEADY

    RING.fade_to(colour, seconds=1.2)
    print("%.1fC %.0f%%RH %.1fhPa" % (temp, hum, pres))
    time.sleep(1.0)

Using the lamp

The colour is a three-hour pressure trend, not a temperature read-out. Give it three hours after the first power-up to learn your room.

Steady bluePressure rising — clear and cooling.
LimeFlat pressure — the next few hours look like the last few.
MagentaPressure dropping fast — expect a change, likely rain.

Tap gestures

GestureResult
1 tapFlash the temperature: one long flash per 10 °C, one short per 1 °C.
2 tapsFlash humidity in the same pattern, in magenta.
Hold 3 sCycle brightness: 35 % → 70 % → 10 %.
Hold 10 sClear the pressure history and re-learn.

Troubleshooting

The five things that go wrong most often, in the order our forum sees them.

Nine times in ten this is a charge-only USB-C cable. Swap for the braided cable in the kit, then check the board's own green power LED under the disc. If that LED is dark, the cable or the port is the problem, not your wiring.

The data wire is on DOUT instead of DIN, or it is on the wrong board pin. Data enters the disc at the arrow marked DIN. Move the white wire to D6 and re-seat.

The sensor is picking up heat from the LEDs or the regulator. Raise it onto the tall standoffs, drop brightness to 20 %, and set SENSOR.offset = -2.0 once the reading stabilises for an hour.

Expected for the first three hours — the history buffer is still filling. After that, a permanently lime lamp usually means the pressure reading is frozen: check for a loose SDA wire and confirm the board sees address 0x76 on the I²C scan.

A crashed sketch can lock the USB drive. Double-tap reset to enter bootloader mode, delete weatherlamp.py, then copy a fresh download and reset once.
