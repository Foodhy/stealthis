Microclimate buffering by canopy density in fragmented temperate forests

L. Okonkwo1, M. Vasquez-Reyes1,2, H. Tanaka3 & E. Bauer2

1 Dept. of Landscape Ecology, Northfield Institute · 2 Centre for Climate Resilience, Univ. of Hallworth · 3 Forest Dynamics Lab, Kyobashi Polytechnic

Abstract

Forest fragmentation alters the thermal environment experienced by understorey organisms. We quantified how canopy leaf-area density attenuates sub-canopy temperature extremes across 42 plots spanning an edge-to-interior gradient.

1. Introduction

Canopy structure is a primary control on the sub-canopy microclimate, mediating both incoming shortwave radiation and turbulent heat exchange. Prior syntheses report buffering of daytime maxima by 2–5 °C beneath dense canopies relative to clearings, yet the dependence of buffering strength on leaf-area density (LAD) remains poorly resolved in fragmented landscapes. Edge effects can penetrate tens of metres into remnant stands, eroding interior conditions, and the thermal safety margins of cold-adapted understorey species are correspondingly narrowed.
