Neural Operators for PDE Surrogate Modeling: A Comparative Survey

  • Tomás Renouf1,2

Abstract

Neural operators learn mappings between function spaces and have emerged as fast surrogates for partial differential equation (PDE) solvers across fluid dynamics, electromagnetics, and climate modeling. This survey organizes the field into three families — spectral, graph/mesh, and attention-based operators — and compares them along discretization invariance, resolution generalization, training cost, and uncertainty quantification. We synthesize results from 84 fictional benchmark studies, identify recurring failure modes on multiscale problems, and outline open challenges in long-horizon rollout stability.

Keywords: neural operators surrogate modeling spectral methods PDE resolution invariance

1 Introduction

Classical numerical solvers discretize a domain and march a fixed mesh forward in time, paying a steep cost when the same family of equations must be solved thousands of times during design optimization or data assimilation. Neural operators instead learn the solution operator G directly, so that a single forward pass approximates the map from input fields (initial conditions, coefficients, boundary data) to the solution field.

The defining promise is discretization invariance: a model trained at one resolution should evaluate consistently at another. Formally, given an input function a and target u, the operator is approximated as

u(x) ≈ Gθ(a)(x) = ∫D κθ(x, y) a(y) dy,
(1)

where κθ is a learned kernel and D the spatial domain. Families of methods differ chiefly in how this integral kernel is parameterized — in the Fourier domain, on a graph, or through attention. Figure 1 organizes the landscape we survey.

3 Spectral Operators

Spectral operators parameterize the kernel in the frequency domain, applying a learned multiplication after a fast transform. The Fourier Neural Operator (FNO) computes each layer as

vt+1(x) = σ (Wvt(x) + ℱ−1(Rθ · ℱ(vt))(x))
(2)

where ℱ is the discrete Fourier transform and Rθ retains only the lowest kmax modes. This truncation yields resolution-invariant inference but limits accuracy on sharp, localized features.

“Mode truncation is both the source of the spectral operator’s efficiency and the origin of its multiscale blind spot.”

Key takeaways

  • Spectral models excel on periodic, structured-grid problems.
  • Boundary-aware variants (SpecBoost) recover most of the error gap on non-periodic domains.
  • Parameter counts stay modest relative to other families.

4 Graph & Mesh Methods

Graph neural operators discretize the kernel integral as message passing over a mesh, making them the natural choice for irregular geometries and adaptive refinement. The trade-off is a quadratic-leaning cost in neighborhood size and sensitivity to mesh quality.

“When the geometry is the problem — airfoils, vasculature, fractured media — mesh-native operators are not optional, they are the baseline.”

Key takeaways

  • Mesh-native operators handle complex geometry where spectral methods cannot.
  • Hierarchical pooling (U-Mesh) stabilizes long rollouts and cuts inference time.
  • Accuracy is tightly coupled to mesh quality and connectivity.

5 Attention Operators

Attention-based operators treat sampled points as tokens and learn the kernel through softmax or linear attention. They generalize across resolutions and naturally express probabilistic outputs, but their compute and memory budgets are the largest in the survey.

6 Open Challenges

Across all three families, three problems recur: long-horizon rollout drift, distribution shift between training and deployment regimes, and the absence of standardized, geometry-rich benchmarks. We argue that progress hinges on physics-consistent training objectives and shared evaluation protocols rather than on architectural novelty alone.

“The field does not need a fourth family of operators so much as it needs benchmarks honest enough to tell the three apart.”

Key takeaways

  • Rollout stability, not single-step accuracy, is the binding constraint.
  • Standardized geometry-rich benchmarks are the most urgent community need.
  • Physics-consistent losses outperform purely data-driven objectives under shift.
