Mitochondrial fission dynamics regulate dendritic spine maturation in cortical neurons under metabolic stress

A. R. Velasco1, J. Okonkwo1,2, M. Lindqvist3, P. S. Raghavan1,✉

1 Dept. of Neurobiology, Brennan Institute, Aotea University · 2 Center for Cellular Bioenergetics, Marwick College · 3 Nordhavn Institute for Systems Biology

Background
Dendritic spine maturation requires sustained local ATP supply, yet how neurons coordinate mitochondrial morphology with synaptic remodelling during metabolic stress remains poorly understood. Aberrant fission has been linked to spine loss across several neurodevelopmental conditions.
Methods
We combined live-cell confocal imaging of primary murine cortical neurons (n = 142 cells, 6 independent cultures) with conditional knockdown of Drp1, fluorescent ATP biosensors, and a glucose-deprivation paradigm. Spine morphology was quantified at 0, 6, 12, 24 h using an automated classifier (Background · Methods · Results · Conclusions structure shown here).
Results
Drp1 knockdown reduced mitochondrial fragmentation by 38.4 ± 4.1% (p < 0.001) and preserved 71% of mushroom-type spines under stress versus 29% in controls. Local ATP indices correlated with spine head volume (r = 0.62, 95% CI 0.51–0.71). The protective effect was abolished by pharmacological uncoupling, indicating a bioenergetic mechanism rather than a structural one.
Conclusions
Constraining mitochondrial fission stabilises the energetic microenvironment needed for spine maturation under metabolic stress. These findings position Drp1 activity as a candidate node for preserving synaptic architecture, and motivate dose-controlled studies of fission modulators in models of energy-limited neurodevelopment.
