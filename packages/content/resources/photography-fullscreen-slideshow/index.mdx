A cinematic, full-viewport slideshow built for photographers who want their work to fill the screen. Each frame crossfades into the next while a slow Ken-Burns transform drifts and zooms the image, so even a still gallery feels alive. A thin uppercase eyebrow, serif title, and metadata line float in a caption card over a soft bottom gradient, keeping text legible without a heavy scrim.

The controls stay out of the way until you need them. A play/pause button carries a live progress ring that fills over each slide's dwell time, and a row of progress dots lets you jump straight to any frame. Everything is keyboard-driven too — left/right arrows step between photos, the spacebar toggles autoplay, and hovering the stage pauses the timer so viewers can linger on a shot.

The layout is fully responsive down to small phones, collapses the metadata gracefully, and respects `prefers-reduced-motion` by disabling the Ken-Burns drift. A small toast confirms actions like pausing or restarting, and all controls expose accessible labels and visible focus rings for AA-level usability.
