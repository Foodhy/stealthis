## Beat-Synced Animation

This Remotion composition renders a fully abstract, beat-driven motion piece at 120 BPM — one beat every 15 frames at 30 fps, totaling 12 beats across the six-second clip. On every beat impact a ring expands outward from center using an `interpolate()` ramp from scale 0.2 to 3.0, fading to transparent as it propagates, while a brief background flash (three-frame opacity burst blended in screen mode) sells the transient energy hit. Eight particle sparks scatter radially from the center on each impact and arc outward with eased distance interpolation, reinforcing the percussive moment before fading out.

Between beats, six rotating squares of varying sizes are placed at different canvas quadrants, each spinning at its own speed with an independent scale-breathing sine wave applied per frame. Three dashed SVG orbit rings drift concentrically around the center, their stroke-dashoffset animated continuously to create a slow mechanical rotation. Eight diagonal accent lines radiate from behind the center, orbiting slowly and pulsing in opacity, built entirely with inline SVG strokes. A `radial-gradient` ambient glow in the background shifts color in sync with the active bar's accent — cycling through purple, cyan, pink, and gold every four beats as the measure progresses.

The center circle is the compositional anchor: it fills with a radial gradient that transitions to the current bar color and receives a `spring()` kick on every beat impact (damping 8, stiffness 280) with the scale momentarily jumping to 1.35 before settling back, overlaid with slow sine-driven ambient breathing. At the bottom, four circular indicator dots correspond to the four 16th-note subdivisions within each beat window; the active subdivision lights up in the bar's accent color with a glow box-shadow, creating a visual metronome strip. The entire scene is framed by corner bracket accents that fade in after frame 20, a thin four-color gradient bottom strip, and a minimal HUD showing the 120 BPM readout in the top-right corner.

> Simulated audio data — waveform values are generated mathematically. No real audio file is required.
