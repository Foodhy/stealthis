Laptop shuts off at 40% battery — bad cell or controller?

RK rk_benchwork ◆ 2,140 rep · asked Jul 21, 2026 · 1,284 views

Client brought in a Nimbus 14 Pro that hard-powers-off — no shutdown sequence, just instant black — whenever the reported charge drops to about 40%. Plugged into the adapter it runs fine for hours under load, so the board itself seems healthy.

Battery is the original 4-cell 52 Wh pack, roughly 620 cycles. Reported design capacity still shows near-new, which is what makes me suspicious of the gas gauge rather than the cells.

  • Cutoff is repeatable within ±2% across six runs.
  • No swelling, no heat, no smell from the pack.
  • Firmware and EC are on the latest release.

Is this a single collapsing cell dragging the pack under the protection threshold, or a fuel-gauge IC that has lost its learned capacity table? What do I measure first?

NC nadia_cells ★ 18,940 rep Battery specialist · Jul 21, 2026

It is almost certainly one collapsing cell, not the gauge. A lost learning table drifts the reading gradually; a weak cell produces exactly what you have — a hard, repeatable cutoff at the same reported percentage because the protection IC trips on per-cell undervoltage while the pack total still looks fine.

Pull the per-cell voltages from the smart battery over SMBus before you open anything:

# read per-cell voltages from the pack controller
fixwell-batt --bus i2c-3 --addr 0x0b cells

cell 1   4.011 V   OK
cell 2   3.987 V   OK
cell 3   3.402 V   <-- 590 mV below siblings
cell 4   4.004 V   OK

A spread over roughly 150 mV under light load already means the pack is unbalanced. Six hundred millivolts is a dead cell in progress. Repeat the read at 60% and again at 45% — the gap will widen sharply just before the cutoff, which is the fingerprint you want.

  1. Log cells at rest, then under a 20 W load.
  2. Note the delta at each step; a growing delta = cell fault.
  3. Flat delta with a jumping percentage = gauge fault.

Replace the pack. Re-celling a bonded pack is possible but the spot-welding and the gauge re-learn are rarely worth the bench hour on a 620-cycle battery.

Before you condemn the pack, rule out the cheap failure: the battery-to-board connector. On this chassis the flex sits directly under the right speaker and takes a thermal cycle every session. A partially lifted contact adds resistance that only matters once current demand rises at lower charge.

fixwell-batt --bus i2c-3 esr --samples 40

pack ESR      184 mOhm   (expected < 90)
connector     ~62 mOhm   suspicious

Reseat the flex, clean both pads with isopropyl, and re-run the discharge. Takes four minutes and saves a pack swap maybe one time in five.

JP jules_pry ● 1,120 rep · Jul 22, 2026

If the per-cell read comes back flat, then it is the gauge and a full calibration cycle is worth one attempt before ordering parts. Charge to 100%, hold two hours, discharge to hardware cutoff with the OS power thresholds disabled, then rest overnight and charge uninterrupted.

fixwell-batt gauge --reset-learned
fixwell-batt gauge --status

learned capacity   4,812 mAh
design capacity    5,000 mAh
qmax valid         no    <-- relearn required

One cycle only. If qmax valid is still no afterwards, the gauge cannot relearn and the pack goes in the recycling bin either way.

Adjacent data point: I saw the identical symptom caused by a hairline crack in the sense resistor joint on the daughterboard. Current sensing went intermittent, the gauge integrated garbage, and the EC pulled the plug at a suspiciously round number every time.

Under magnification the joint looked perfect; only a four-wire measurement showed it drifting. Worth twenty seconds with the meter once the pack is exonerated.
