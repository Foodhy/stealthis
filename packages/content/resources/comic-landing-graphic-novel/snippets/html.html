The Story

Two hands, one map, a border that keeps moving.

In the last winter of the Ashfall War, a military cartographer named Sable is ordered to redraw a border that no one believes in anymore. Her only correspondent is Cael — a deserter she has never met — whose letters arrive folded inside the maps she is meant to correct.

What begins as coordinates becomes confession. As the front collapses and the ash keeps falling, the two strangers build a country out of paper: a place that exists only in the margins, in the things they cannot say to anyone else.

Painted across 312 pages in muted umber and dusk-blue, The Ashfall Letters is a quiet epic about distance — the kind you can map, and the kind you cannot.

From the Author

A note from E. R. Vale

“I started The Ashfall Letters as a single drawing: two hands holding the same map from opposite edges. Everything else — the war, the winter, the years of correspondence — grew out of the question that drawing asked. Who do we become when the only honest country we have is the one we write to a stranger?”

“I painted every page by hand in muted earths, because I wanted the book to feel like something recovered rather than invented — a bundle of letters someone kept too long.”

— E. R. Vale, 2026
