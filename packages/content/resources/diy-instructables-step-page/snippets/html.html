Build a folding camp stool from one board

Eight chapters, one 1×6 board, and a handful of hardware. Cut, drill, pivot, sand, oil — you end up with a stool that folds flat enough to slide behind a car seat.

01

Supplies

One 1×6×8′ board is enough for the whole stool with an offcut to spare. Everything else fits in a sandwich bag. Tick things off as you gather them — the counter at the top of the card keeps score.

Shopping checklist

Tools

  • Handsaw or mitre saw
  • Drill + 8 mm brad point
  • Clamps ×2
  • Random-orbit sander
  • Combination square
  • 13 mm spanner
TIP

Buy the board a day early and stand it indoors overnight. Store-warm ash moves as it dries and a bowed leg ruins the fold.

Cut list

Six pieces, two lengths. Mark them all before you cut anything — a single pass with the square saves you from the classic mistake of trimming a long leg twice.

03

Drilling

The pivot hole sits exactly at the midpoint of each leg, 38 mm in from the edge. Clamp all four legs together and drill once — identical holes matter more than perfect holes.

Run the drill at a slow speed and back the bit out twice to clear chips. Ash burnishes quickly and a glazed hole grips the bolt in a way you will notice for years.

TIP

Slip a scrap under the stack. Tear-out on the exit face is the only thing that shows once the stool is oiled.

04

Assembly

Stack washer, leg, washer, leg, washer on each bolt, then tighten the lock nut until the frame swings under its own weight and stops where you leave it. That is the whole trick.

  1. Dry-fit both frames and open them fully on a flat floor.
  2. Glue and clamp the seat rails to the outer frame only.
  3. Torque the lock nuts to finger-tight plus a quarter turn.
  4. Fold and unfold twenty times, then re-check the nuts.
CARE

Over-tightening crushes the nylon washers and the stool goes from smooth to sticky. Loose beats tight here.

05

Sanding

Three grits, no shortcuts: 120 to flatten the saw marks, 180 to erase the 120, 240 to make the oil sit evenly. Break every edge with a folded sheet — a 1 mm chamfer stops splinters where hands land.

06

Finish

Hard-wax oil, wiped on thin with a lint-free rag and buffed back after ten minutes. Two coats with a light 400-grit rub between them. Thin coats cure; thick coats stay tacky for a week.

TIP

Lay oily rags flat outdoors to dry. Bunched up they can self-heat — dispose of them the way the tin tells you.

07

Testing

Load it before you trust it. Sit, shift your weight side to side, then stand on the seat rails near the pivots. If anything creaks, it is almost always a rail that did not get enough clamp pressure.

08

Final

Folded it is 65 mm thick and weighs 1.9 kg. Mine lives behind the passenger seat and has done two seasons of riverbank coffee without a single re-tighten.

If you build one, change something. Longer legs for a camp kitchen, a webbing seat instead of canvas, a rope handle through the rail. Post it below — variations are the best part of the thread.
