A Light on the Water

Maple had never sailed at night before, and the river was wider than any she had crossed in daylight. She tucked her paws beneath her tail and watched the moon balance on the very tip of her little paper boat, as if it might tumble in and float away like a coin.

“Don’t worry,” whispered the smallest firefly, settling on the rim of her ear. “The moon knows the way home. All we have to do is keep our lantern bright and follow where the ripples shine.”

So Maple held the lantern a little higher, and one by one the fireflies blinked awake along the reeds — a tiny golden trail stitched across the dark water, leading her gently toward the far, sleeping shore.
