Abstract

Background
Maintaining quantum coherence in solid-state lattices beyond the microsecond regime remains a central obstacle for scalable qubit arrays.
Methods
We fabricated 24 strained tin–selenide (SnSe) thin films and measured spin–echo coherence times T2 across a temperature sweep from 12 mK to 4.2 K.
Results
Biaxial strain of 0.84 % extended T2 by a factor of 3.7×, reaching 412 µs at 18 mK.
Conclusion
Controlled lattice strain provides a reproducible route to long-lived coherence without isotopic purification.

Keywords: quantum coherence cryogenic lattices tin selenide spin–echo strain engineering

1 Introduction

Solid-state qubits offer a compelling pathway toward fault-tolerant quantum computation, yet their utility is bounded by decoherence arising from hyperfine coupling and phonon-mediated relaxation [1]. Prior work established that isotopic purification can suppress nuclear-spin noise, but the fabrication overhead limits throughput [2].

Here we investigate whether mechanical strain alone can stabilize coherence in unpurified tin–selenide films. Strain modifies the local phonon density of states and lifts orbital degeneracies, both of which influence the dephasing channel [3]. We hypothesize that a narrow band of biaxial strain maximizes T2 without inducing structural disorder [4].

2 Methods

Films were grown by molecular-beam epitaxy on lattice-mismatched substrates to induce controlled biaxial straina. Coherence was probed with a Hahn spin–echo sequence at a Larmor frequency of 6.83 GHz.

4 Discussion

Our findings indicate that strain engineering offers a scalable alternative to isotopic purification. The stretch exponent β trends toward unity at optimal strain, suggesting a more homogeneous dephasing environment [7]. Beyond the optimum, the abrupt decline correlates with the onset of microcracking observed under cryogenic microscopy [8].

These results align with theoretical predictions of strain-modulated phonon bottlenecks while highlighting the practical fabrication window that future device pipelines must respect.

5 Conclusion

Controlled biaxial strain near 0.84 % reproducibly extends quantum coherence in cryogenic tin–selenide lattices by nearly fourfold. The approach requires no isotopic purification and is compatible with standard epitaxial growth, offering a tractable path toward denser qubit arrays.
