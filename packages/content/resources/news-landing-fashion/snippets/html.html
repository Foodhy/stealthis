Wearing the Void

01 / 09

A season stripped to its silhouette. We photograph absence as a garment — light, weight and shadow rendered as couture.

An Editor's Note

There is a discipline in restraint that the runway has spent a decade forgetting. This issue is our argument for the empty frame — the collar left undone, the page left mostly white, the model asked to stand and simply be present. We arrived at the studio with forty looks and used eleven. What remained felt heavier for what was taken away.

The houses we follow this season have converged, almost without conferring, on a monochrome grammar: charcoal, bone, the particular grey of wet pavement at six in the morning. Colour appears once, like a held breath — a single seam of acid light traced along a hem, a glove, a closing look. We have let that logic govern these pages.

“To dress in nothing is the hardest tailoring of all — it leaves the body no place to hide.”

— Solène Marchetti, atelier director

Manifesto

Less, until it
aches.

We believe the empty frame is not a failure of nerve but a decision. We believe a single colour, used once, says more than a spectrum.

We believe a garment should survive being photographed in silence. We believe negative space is the most expensive material in the room.

We believe the reader deserves room to breathe. We print on heavy stock and leave the margins wide. This is the whole of our credo.
