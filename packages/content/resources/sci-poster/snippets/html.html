Tidal Forcing Governs Subsurface Microplastic Transport Across the Vellmar Shelf

E. R. Saldívar1, M. Þorsteinsdóttir2, J. K. Owusu-Banahene1, L. Castellanos1,2, & H. Nakamura2

1Meridian Ocean Institute, Dept. of Physical Oceanography · 2Kaelrwood University, Coastal Systems Lab

Poster #B-217 Session B · Wed 16:30

01 Introduction

Microplastic (MP) particles < 5 mm accumulate in coastal sediments, yet the physical drivers of their vertical redistribution remain poorly constrained. Prevailing models treat the seabed as a passive sink, neglecting tidal pumping through permeable shelf sands.

We hypothesise that semidiurnal tidal forcing modulates pore-water advection sufficiently to mobilise buoyant MP fragments to depths exceeding 12 cm. The Vellmar Shelf—a well-mixed, sandy embayment—offers an ideal natural laboratory.

  • Q1. Does tidal phase predict MP concentration at depth?
  • Q2. What is the characteristic mobilisation timescale?
  • Q3. Can a 1-D advection–diffusion model reproduce observed profiles?

02 Methods

Sediment cores (n = 48) were collected over 6 tidal cycles at 3 cross-shelf stations using a hand-driven push corer. Cores were sectioned at 2 cm intervals; MP fragments isolated by density separation (ZnCl2, ρ = 1.6 g cm-3) and counted under fluorescence.

03 Results

MP concentration at 10–12 cm depth tracked tidal phase with a lag of ~2.4 h (r = 0.81, p < 0.001). Flood tides drove a 3.4× enrichment relative to slack water.

04 Conclusion

Tidal pumping is a first-order control on MP burial in permeable shelf sediments. A simple 1-D advection–diffusion model (Eq. 1) reproduced 87% of observed depth-profile variance.

Coastal MP budgets that ignore tidal advection may underestimate deep-sediment storage by up to one order of magnitude. Future work will extend sampling to muddy substrates.
