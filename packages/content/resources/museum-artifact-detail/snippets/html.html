Curatorial note

Among the most complete survivals from the Kestharn workshops, this vessel was almost certainly made for the spring tide-blessing — a coastal rite in which offerings of grain and salt were carried to the water's edge. The cresting-wave band, drawn in three diminishing registers, is a signature of Oríel's late hand.

The celadon pooling at the shoulder, once read as a firing flaw, is now understood as a deliberate effect: glaze deliberately thinned so that the wave motif would appear to surface from beneath still water. Comparable fragments in Gallery 14 help date the piece to the final decade of the workshop's activity.
